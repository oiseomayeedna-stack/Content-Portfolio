10 Things AI Teams Get Wrong About Web Data
A sample top 10 list written as a content example for AI infrastructure audiences.
Most AI teams spend months on models and days on the data feeding them. These are the mistakes that show up most often when that balance is wrong.
1. Treating data collection as a one-off project. Web data changes constantly. A pipeline built once and never revisited quietly goes stale.
2. Ignoring freshness. A model grounded in last quarter's web is answering today's questions with old information. Decide how fresh your data needs to be, then build to that.
3. Chasing volume over quality. More pages are not better data. Duplicates, boilerplate, and low-quality sources dilute what the model learns.
4. Underestimating reliability at scale. Collecting from a handful of pages is easy. Collecting reliably from thousands, across sites that change layouts and defend against automation, is an infrastructure problem.
5. Running pipelines without monitoring. If nobody knows a source broke on Tuesday, bad data flows downstream until someone notices the output got worse.
6. Skipping the compliance conversation. What you collect, how you collect it, and how you use it all matter. Bring legal and security in early, not after launch.
7. Forgetting provenance. If you can't say where a piece of data came from, you can't debug it, audit it, or remove it when you need to.
8. Underbudgeting infrastructure costs. Bandwidth, storage, and retries add up quickly. The cheap prototype rarely predicts the production bill.
9. Building everything in-house by default. Owning your core model makes sense. Owning every layer of data collection often doesn't. Decide what is a differentiator and what is plumbing.
10. Never measuring the effect on the model. The real test of a data source is whether it improves outputs. Without evaluation, you're guessing which data is worth the cost.

The takeaway: data infrastructure rewards the teams that treat it as a product, with owners, monitoring, and metrics, not as a task to finish and forget.
